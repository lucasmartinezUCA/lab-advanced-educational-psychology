The Hidden Dimension: One Question, Three Kinds of Evidence

One question runs through this lab: does raising students’ motivation raise their grades? You will examine it through a correlational survey, a classroom comparison and a research synthesis, then decide what the combined evidence can support.

A strong correlation

A district survey of 120 students measured motivation and grades. From the front, each dot is one student and the cloud rises steadily to the right.

Before you explore: if you compared only students who started the year at a similar level, what would happen to this link?

Now rotate the cloud. A third variable is hiding in its depth. Turn the scene until you can see it.

The depth axis is prior achievement. Students who started ahead tend to be more motivated and to earn higher grades. The darker the dot, the higher the student’s starting point.

What this design can tell you
The strength and direction of an association, and where to look next.
What it cannot tell you
What would happen if a teacher actually raised motivation. A third variable, or reverse causation, may sit behind the pattern.

Two classes, one program

A school pilots a motivation program in Class A, its advanced track. Class B continues as usual. Each column is a student’s score; the dot on top shows prior achievement.

When the school compares final scores, how much of the gap will the program explain?

    What this design can tell you
    With chance assignment, a causal effect of the program in these conditions, give or take sampling noise.
    What it cannot tell you
    Whether the same effect would appear in another school, age group or setting.

    What the literature says

    A review pools published evaluations of similar programs in four settings. Each sphere is a study: further right means a larger effect, higher up means a larger sample, and bigger spheres carry more weight.

    Some studies were never published. The box beneath the plot holds them. What will the pooled effect do when they are added?

    What this design can tell you
    The expected size of an effect and how much it varies across studies and settings.
    What it cannot tell you
    More than its studies allow. Missing studies and mixed settings shape the average.
